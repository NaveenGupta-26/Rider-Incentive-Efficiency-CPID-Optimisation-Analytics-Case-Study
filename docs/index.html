Phase 01 / The Problem

The Money Nobody Is
Counting Correctly.

Every last-mile platform (Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit) spends Rs200-500 Cr per quarter on rider incentives. Most measure success by total deliveries during a bonus week. This is a causal fallacy.

The "Always-On" Waste

87% of deliveries would have happened anyway. By paying flat bonuses to everyone, platforms subsidize existing behavior while overpaying for new supply. Riders who are online every peak hour regardless of bonuses represent pure cost leakage.

0.98× ROI

Our analysis revealed that the industry-standard "Flat Per-Order" bonus has an ROI below 1.0. The platform recovers less than Rs1 for every Rs1 spent on these bonuses. It is loss-making at the margin.

Phase 02 / Metric Design

The North Star:
CPID vs. Baseline.

CPID = Total Bonus Paid ÷ Incremental Deliveries

Incremental Deliveries = (Deliveries with Bonus) – (Rider's Personal Baseline)

Meet Ramesh — The Case for CPID

Ramesh normally does 18 deliveries a day. Today, a streak bonus pushes him to 22. The old way counts 22 deliveries. The CPID way counts 4.

That 5× difference in measurement is where hundreds of crores disappear annually. Isolating the marginal delivery is the only way to measure true incentive elasticity.

  • Baseline Rider-specific counterfactual based on non-incentive weeks.
  • Supply Funnel Surge acts at the 'Online' stage; Streak acts at the 'Completion' stage.
Phase 03 / SQL Analysis

8 Queries to Uncover
the Unit Economics.

Query 2: Core CPID by Incentive Type
WITH baseline AS (
  SELECT rider_id, AVG(deliveries_completed) AS avg_baseline
  FROM shifts WHERE incentive_type = 'none'
  GROUP BY rider_id
),
incentive_weeks AS (
  SELECT s.incentive_type,
    SUM(s.deliveries_completed - b.avg_baseline) AS incr_deliveries,
    SUM(s.incentive_pay) AS total_spend
  FROM shifts s JOIN baseline b ON b.rider_id = s.rider_id
  GROUP BY s.incentive_type
)
SELECT incentive_type, 
  ROUND(total_spend / NULLIF(incr_deliveries, 0), 1) AS cpid_rs
FROM incentive_weeks;

Phase 04 / Visual Analysis

Quantifying the Gaps.

Isolating the Efficiency Leader

Surge Top-ups are 37% more efficient than industry-standard Flat bonuses. By rewarding 'Online Presence' rather than every individual delivery, we filter out the always-on subsidy.

Finding: Flat bonuses often cost >Rs90 per incremental delivery, exceeding the Rs74 revenue ceiling.

The 2–6 Month Sweet Spot

Elasticity follows a U-curve. New riders (0-2mo) are still learning; veterans (12+mo) have fixed schedules. The 2-6 month cohort represents the most elastic supply segment.

Strategy: Aggressively target "Mid-Tenure" riders with streak bonuses to maximize volume.

Geography as a Decision Matrix

Tier-1 metros absorb 34% of budget but suffer from extreme supply saturation. Conversely, Tier-3 cities respond 2.5× more efficiently to the same spend.

Recommendation: Reallocate 15% of Tier-1 discretionary spend to Tier-2/3 "Growth Hubs".
Phase 05 / A/B Experiment

Validation via Cluster
Randomisation.

The SUTVA Challenge

Randomizing by rider in the same city causes spillover (Rider A's bonus takes Rider B's orders). We implemented Cluster Randomisation at the City Level to isolate treatment effects cleanly.

The Pure Surge Failure

Phase 6 simulation showed that a pure surge model maximized efficiency but reduced total rider earnings by Rs110/wk. We rejected this in favor of a Hybrid model.

Phase 06 / Strategic Recommendation

The Path to Rs60 Cr Savings.

Efficiency is not about cutting; it's about reallocation. By moving from a one-size-fits-all model to a tenure-x-tier matrix, we improve the platform's bottom line while increasing rider stability.
